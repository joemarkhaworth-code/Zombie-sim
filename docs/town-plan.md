# Brackenby: the town plan (draft for approval)

One fixed town, the same every run (CONFIG.world.seed), designed by hand like a real place rather than generated
differently each time. This is the plan for step 13. Map: [town-plan.png](town-plan.png). Working names only:
Brackenby (the town) and the River Kell.

## What it's drawn from

Researched from the councils' own character appraisals (sources at the end).

- **Knaresborough.** The castle sits on a high promontory above the Nidd gorge. The market place lies between the High
  Street and the castle, and the High Street slopes down to Bond End and the river. There are four historic stone
  bridges (High Bridge, Low Bridge, Grimbald Bridge, and a wooden footbridge upstream) and a castellated four-arch
  railway viaduct across the gorge. The station is by the High Street, with the line in a tunnel under the town.
  - The river side: Castle Mill and its 40 m weir. Waterside runs below the crag with boat hire, cafés and the
    World's End pub, reached by steps (Gallon Steps). There's an old gasworks, Abbey Road (a rural lane with no
    pavements), and Mother Shipton's cave on the far bank.
  - Around the old core:
    - an industrial triangle between two roads and the river (metal sheds, car dealers, a brewery, the sewage works,
      the football ground, caravan sites on the low ground by the river);
    - the 1970s Eastfield estate, whose cul-de-sacs are joined by ginnels, with open-plan front gardens, a Co-op on
      its green and a dike that floods;
    - 1936 rows of 2-bed terraces with date plaques, built for rent;
    - a 4-acre allotment field by the railway, the King George V playing field and a 1930s parade, "Top Shops";
    - the 1876 cemetery, a swimming pool, the school and the cricket club on the crag top;
    - the stockbroker belt in walled grounds on Ripley Road;
    - the hamlet of Calcutt across the river, with its pub on the junction, a chapel and cricket in an old quarry.
- **Wetherby.** The plan is "typical of a market town of early foundation": a large triangular market place, infilled
  over time, with the Town Hall standing alone in its middle and the Shambles (a covered market). Roads enter its north
  end from the north, east and west, and the single bridge to the south controls everything; the descent to the bridge
  is steep and bends. The weir and old mill are about 100 yards upstream.
  - The streets: a winding Back Lane, and yards behind the frontages ("a significant feature"). Three-storey
    buildings line the busy streets, two-storey ones the quiet streets.
  - The coaching town: fifteen inns on the Great North Road, with archways to their yards.
  - Growth: the 1876 railway brought middle-class terraces north of the town, with bay windows and low front walls.
    There's the 1915 cinema, the workhouse that became a hospital (closed 1993), the racecourse out on the York road
    (from 1891), the water meadows (the ings) across the river, and a bandstand at the foot of the bridge.
- **Haworth.** The village is built on a slope: a steep, setted Main Street twists up from the mill by the beck to the
  church, the parsonage and a triangular top square, where the Black Bull is set at an angle to the church steps.
  - Folds: small setted squares of cottages.
  - Weavers' cottages with long rows of top-floor windows; three-storey Victorian shops lower down; the 1897
    three-storey Co-op at the bottom.
  - A 1910 park linking the village to the station, and mill workers' terraces across the beck.
  - Stone slate roofs stepping down the hill, with the moor right behind.
- **The general pattern of English towns.** They grow in rings: the medieval core with burgage plots, yards and
  ginnels, then the Victorian fringe, the interwar and postwar estates, and the bypass with its industry and
  superstore. The rings are lopsided: growth follows the railway, and the river holds it back.

## The plan

The map is the real 25 x 25 screen grid (each screen 11 x 15 tiles, stepping 7 across and 11 down: 179 x 279 tiles).
North is up.

**The river and the gorge.** The River Kell comes in from the moor on the west and runs east through a gorge with crag
sides. Below the castle it bends sharply south-east (as the Wharfe does at Wetherby) and opens out into water meadows,
then runs past the industrial estate and out to the south-east.

- **Seven ways across.** These are what step 20's wellies and the run seed's blocked bridges play with:
  - a wooden footbridge in the gorge woods;
  - the Old Bridge, medieval and 3 tiles wide, carrying the main road (Knaresborough's High Bridge);
  - the railway viaduct, high, with the stopped train on it: go through the carriages or along the ledge beside them;
  - the mill weir (a dare);
  - Low Bridge, at the foot of Briggate;
  - East Bridge, on the south-east road;
  - the concrete bypass bridge.
- **The ford.** There's also a ford in the meadows: the shallows, where you need wellies.
- **Crashed cars.** The run seed blocks some road bridges with them, so the safe way home changes from run to run.

**Town centre (most zombies, best loot).** It sits on the ridge on the north bank:

- **The Market Place:** a big infilled triangle with the old Town Hall standing alone in the middle and the Shambles
  down one side. The High Street runs along its north side, and the castle ruins are at its south tip, on the crag
  above the gorge (Knaresborough's arrangement, with Wetherby's market place).
- **The High Street** runs east-west with three-storey shops: the post office (and its queue), two banks, the chemist
  and the newsagent. Behind the frontages are burgage plots with yards and ginnels, and a winding Back Lane with the
  Methodist chapel.
- **The coaching inn**, the Angel, has an archway to its stable yard.
- **The cinema (1915)**, the police and fire stations, and the 1897 Co-op stand along the High Street's east end.
- **The west end is Haworth's:** St John's and its graveyard at the top, the Black Bull at an angle to the church steps,
  and the steep, setted Bond End dropping to the Old Bridge.
- **The station** is just north of the High Street, with the line in a tunnel under the town, coming out onto the
  viaduct.
- **The stopped train (decided: the railway is open in 1991).** A passenger train stopped across the viaduct on the
  bank holiday weekend: its front carriages are out over the gorge and the rear ones are still in the tunnel mouth.
  - **Narrow and dangerous.** The carriages are true scale: 3 tiles wide with their walls, so inside there's a one-tile
    aisle between the seats. Zombie passengers are still in their seats, shut in behind the doors. Open one and they
    come at you one at a time down the aisle: manageable, but there's nowhere to step aside and no way round.
    Outside, there's only a one-tile ledge between the train and the viaduct's parapet, high above the river. Getting
    across the viaduct means going through the train or along that ledge.
  - **High rewards.** The luggage racks are full of day-trippers' holiday bags: suitcases, holdalls and handbags with
    food, flasks, first aid and odd finds, maybe a bigger rucksack (more room in your bag). There's a buffet car with
    tea and crisps, and the guard's van at the back has a locked cage of parcels and post sacks.
- **Waterside,** below the crag and reached by steps: the boat landing, a café, the riverside pub, cottages, Castle Mill
  and its weir, and the old gasworks by Low Bridge.

**Inner town (Victorian).**

- **North of the centre, towards the railway:** stone terraces with bay windows and low front walls, back-to-backs with
  back alleys, the board school, a Victorian park with a bandstand, the allotments by the railway, and the Grange (the
  old workhouse, now a hospital).
- **West, in the gorge bottom:** the mill quarter, with Low Mill by the river, weavers' cottages with long windows, folds
  and mill terraces.
- **East:** Park Row's old cottages, built of stone robbed from the castle, and the 1936 terraces with date plaques.

**Suburbs.**

- **North-east, across the railway:** 1930s-40s brick semis and council houses, the "Top Shops" parade and the King
  George V playing field.
- **East:** the 1970s estate, cul-de-sacs joined by ginnels (a maze to get lost in), with a small Co-op on its green.
- **South-east, on the crag top inside the river bend:** 1950s-70s houses and bungalows, the swimming baths (the
  leisure centre), the 1876 cemetery, the high school and the cricket club.
- **North-west:** 1940s semis and 1960s estates.
- **West, towards the moor road:** big houses in walled grounds, and the Hall by the river with its crazy golf.
- **Across the river:** the hamlet up the hill, with its pub on the junction, a chapel and cricket in the old quarry.

**Edge of town (quiet, sparse, good for foraging and farming).**

- **East:** the industrial triangle between the two roads and the river (brewery, car dealers, sheds, sewage works,
  football ground), the caravan park by the river, and the 1980s bypass curving round the south and east, with the new
  superstore (opened 1990) at its roundabout.
- **North:** farmland, with the golf course to the north-west and the racecourse and grandstand to the north-east.
- **West:** the moor.
- **South and south-west:** woods (with the scout hut and archery range: bows and arrows), the old quarry, and farms
  with barns.

## Scale: small outside, big inside (Pokémon-style)

At true scale (1 tile is about 1.5 m) the whole map is only about 270 m x 420 m, while Knaresborough and Wetherby are
each 2-3 km across. The fix (decided in playtesting) is Pokémon's: buildings are smaller outside than inside.

- **Outside, on the town map,** each building is a shrunk token, about half true size: a terraced house is 3 x 3, a pub
  6 x 5, the church 6 x 9, a train carriage 2 x 8 (full table in CLAUDE.md, "Scale").
- **Inside, on separate interior screens,** everything is true scale: a terraced house is a full screen of rooms, and a
  carriage is a long screen with its one-tile aisle.

So the town map fits a proper town's worth of streets: terrace rows of 8-12 houses rather than 3, several shops on each
side of the High Street, and real estates of cul-de-sacs. The countryside at the edge is compressed further.

Walking: from the market to the edge of town is about 85 steps east or west (~3 game hours) and about 130 steps north
or south (~4.5 hours). That's a real trip, with a reason to find a safe house out there before dark.

## Questions for you

1. **Where do you start?** I've proposed your own terraced house in the inner town north of the market (the pink box),
   not the Market Place itself, which should be the most dangerous place on the map.
2. **Names.** "Brackenby" and "River Kell" are placeholders. Street names would follow the real pattern: High Street,
   Market Place, Back Lane, Bond End, Briggate, Kirkgate, Waterside, Station Road, York Road, North Road.
3. ~~The railway~~ Decided: open in 1991, with a passenger train stopped across the viaduct (see the town centre).
